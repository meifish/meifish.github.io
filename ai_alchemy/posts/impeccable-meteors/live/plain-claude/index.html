01 — The shower

Comet dust, on fire.

Every October, Earth sweeps through the debris trail Halley’s Comet leaves behind. Grains no bigger than sand hit our upper atmosphere and flare into streaks of light: the Orionids.

1P

Halley’s Comet

The source. Its dust has been drifting along its orbit for centuries; we cross it twice a year.

~20/hr

Meteors per hour

A steady rate under truly dark skies, with the occasional bright fireball and lingering train.

66km/s

Very fast

Among the swiftest showers of the year. Blink and they’re gone, so keep your eyes up.

Oct 21–22

Peak night

The shower runs for weeks, but activity climbs to its maximum around the 21st and 22nd.

The meteors seem to fly out of a single point in the sky, the radiant, which sits near the raised club of Orion, the Hunter. That’s where the shower gets its name. Orion clears the eastern horizon late in the evening and climbs high by the small hours, which is why the best show comes after midnight.

You don’t need to stare at the radiant itself. Meteors appear all over the sky; trace their paths backward and they all point home to Orion. Our volunteers will be on hand with green laser pointers to show you where to look.

02 — When & where

One long night, moon to dawn.

Come early to settle in and let your eyes adapt. A waxing moon lights the early evening; once it sets around 1 am, the sky goes properly dark and the meteors take over until first light.

  1. 8:00 pm

    Gates open

    Park in the lower lot, follow the red lanterns to Meadow Field, claim your patch of grass.

  2. 9:00 pm

    Sky tour

    Laser-guided walk through the autumn constellations, plus telescopes on the moon and Saturn.

  3. 11:00 pm

    Cocoa & talks

    Short talk on Halley’s Comet by the fire circle. Hot drinks while they last.

  4. ~1:00 am

    Moonset — prime time

    The waxing moon dips below the ridge. Dark skies, Orion high, meteors at their best.

    Best viewing
  5. ~6:30 am

    Dawn

    Twilight washes out the last meteors. Stay for sunrise over the hollow, or head home happy.

Getting there

Cedar Hollow Dark-Sky Park

A sheltered valley ringed by cedar ridges, far from city glow. Meadow Field is a ten-minute walk from the lower lot along a gently graded, lantern-lit trail.

  • Arrive with dimmed lights. Switch to parking lights when you pass the park sign so you don’t blind early arrivals.
  • Meadow Field, Cedar Hollow Dark-Sky Park. Follow the red lanterns from the lower lot.
  • Come and go freely. Gates stay open all night; leave whenever you like.
  • Cloudy night? We’ll post a go / no-go by 5 pm on Oct 21 and email everyone who RSVPs.
03 — Pack list

Dress for 4 am, not 8 pm.

October nights in the hollow drop close to freezing, and you’ll be lying still for hours. Here’s what makes the difference between a magical night and a shivering one.

Warm layers

Thermals, a puffy, hat, gloves and thick socks. Bring one more layer than you think you need.

Recliner or blanket

A zero-gravity chair or a thick blanket and pad. Lying back lets you take in the whole sky.

Red flashlight

Red light keeps everyone’s night vision intact. No red light? A strip of red cellophane works.

Thermos

Something hot to sip makes 3 am feel cozy. We’ll have refills of cocoa at the fire circle.

Give it 20 minutes for your eyes to fully dark-adapt Phones on red mode or tucked away No white lights on the field, please Leave no trace: pack out what you pack in
